# Creative Intelligence — research → packaging → hook → story → retention → learning

**Purpose:** help ContentGen and coding agents systematically create differentiated, compelling, evidence-backed long/short explainers while preserving a premium BrandKit. Optimize **viewer satisfaction, meaningful watch time and sustainable profit**, not a fictional "viral probability." This document is the normative **creative planning and measurement contract**. Time-sensitive examples live in [the dated 2026-10-21 market scan](research/2026-10-21-video-market-scan.md). Owner boundaries: [Brand Studio](12-brand-studio.md), [audio](13-audio-production.md), [Kumiki](14-kumiki-integration.md).

## 1. One closed-loop production pipeline

\`MarketSignalSnapshot → OpportunityBrief → PackagingHypotheses (title + thumbnail + opening promise) → EvidencePlan → StoryPlan (long or short) → SemanticStoryboard → BrandKit/Platform adaptation → Kumiki scene compilation + Fish/local audio → deterministic QA + human approval → publish → OutcomeObservation → ExperimentLearning\`.

The **packaging promise is authored before the script**. Titles, thumbnails, 0–30s introduction and final payoff must share the same approved answer/claim IDs. "This title gets 8M views" is not a causal claim about the words or color. Popularity is affected by channel distribution, topic urgency, existing fans, timing, recommendation traffic and unobserved retention.

Every downstream artifact pins: channel/brand, evidence IDs and observation date, script/brand/style revision, title and thumbnail hypothesis IDs, rendering assets and rights, output profile, and experimentally measured results (or explicitly **unknown**).

**Readiness gates:**
- Can the viewer recognize their problem or a compelling mystery immediately?
- Is the title promise answerable from *actual* verified evidence, not exaggerated?
- Does the first visual/voice event substantively deliver on the packaging promise?
- Does every beat explain, test, compare, or change viewer understanding?
- Does the film deliver a concrete result and limitation?
- Are brand, font, audio, music, illustrations and source rights resolved?
- Has a representative full-length production been viewed by a human? Deterministic rules cannot certify aesthetics or predict virality.

## 2. Demand intelligence, not trend-copying

\`MarketSignalSnapshot\`: \`sourceUrl, provider, topic, observedAt, windowStart/End, audienceMarket, format, sourceType, measured|estimated|unknown, rawValue, sampleDescription, confidence, rightsScope\`.

**Gather affordable, traceable demand signals** from YouTube Studio Trends (own audience/searches), public channel/video pages, Google Trends (relative search interest, not absolute search volume), platform topic updates, dated reliable surveys, source-backed news, user questions/comments and optional creator analytics when authorized. Collect public competitor video title, URL/video ID, published date, format, duration, observed public views at a specific timestamp, channel subscribers when available, topical category, and snapshot reference to publicly observed thumbnail. Do not infer CTR, AVD, impressions, revenue, or experiment winners from public view count.

**Prioritize channels/topics similar to the proposed audience.** Contrast at least several recent videos in the same category and format, including underperformers, not just giant success stories. Compute a **descriptive outlier ratio** = public views / observed channel median of a defined, comparable sample. Normalize by upload age or compare matched age windows if data exist. Reject naive ratios mixing Shorts with long-form, mixing 3-day uploads with 3-year uploads, or using views without a measurement date. If only lifetime counts exist, disclose exposure bias. Do not imply that 3× channel median guarantees repeatability.

**Opportunity evaluation:** user concern intensity; personal stakes/savings; evidence availability and freshness; clear visual teaching mechanism; originality/new information; format fit; production cost; channel expertise/brand fit; advertiser-safe monetization; shelf life; competitive saturation. Return *reasons, uncertainties and source links*, not an unverified 0–100 viral score. Keep a mixed portfolio: timely interest + durable evergreen lessons + experimentally distinctive concepts.

**Copyright and originality:** study patterns and public metadata, not copy scripts, narration, thumbnails, proprietary artwork or distinctive animations. YouTube/Meta monetization policies disfavor repetitive, low-value, lightly modified or mass-produced videos; **automation is for original production infrastructure**.

## 3. Packaging compiler — creative hypotheses

Produce **three materially different** title+thumbnail+opening hypotheses, not the same title with different colors. Each variant records:
- audience need and emotional motive (save money, avoid mistake, curiosity, fear resolved, delight, status/competence)
- factual promise, required evidence and exact eventual payoff; risk flags on disputed claims
- title (plain/searchable) and thumbnail's **complementary** visual question (not merely repeat title text)
- visible subject, contrast pair, one visual contradiction/number/result, semantic palette from BrandKit, aspect and mobile-safe crop
- hook script + first visual event tied to promise; approach class and intended mood
- original assets + licensing; brand tokens; editability and independent variant IDs

### High-utility title families (formats, not guarantees)

| Class | Shape | Appropriate use / guard |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden cost | \`The Real [Timeframe] Cost of [Recognizable Decision]\` | Show complete assumptions, regional/range sensitivity and alternatives |
| Cost breakdown | \`Why [Everyday Item/Repair] Costs So Much\` | Mechanistic cost waterfall, not "corporate greed" without evidence |
| Contradiction | \`Why [Expected Outcome] Doesn't Happen\` | Make premise observable and resolve nuance honestly |
| Head-to-head | \`[A] vs [B]: Which Actually Costs Less?\` | Apples-to-apples scenario model and break-even threshold |
| Everyday mystery | \`Why Does [Familiar Object] [Odd Behavior]?\` | Show real physical causal mechanism |
| Risk/escape | \`The [Mistake/Cost] Most [Audience] Miss\` | No unsupported "everyone" or invented loss |
| Reality check | \`Is [Popular Solution] Actually Worth It?\` | State where it wins **and** loses |
| Concrete reversal | \`[New Evidence] Changes What We Thought About [X]\` | Actual new development, source date and confidence |
| Counterintuitive mechanism | \`The [Unexpected] Engineering Behind [Familiar Thing]\` | Show transformation/hidden subsystem clearly |

Do **not** universally enforce "Why", "The Real Reason", all-caps, exclamation marks, fear or curiosity gaps. Channel and audience language matters. Claims like "\`Only\`", "\`Secret\`", "\`Never\`", "\`Proven\`" require precise support.

### Premium thumbnail design contract

A thumbnail is an **information hierarchy**, not a palette trick. At a small preview size, validate one focal subject, at most one dominant tension/contrast or payoff, deliberately minimal text (often 0–5 words; heuristic), correctly legible large numerals and a recognizable brand accent/type system. Thumbnail copy complements the title; avoid repeating the entire headline. Preserve face/subject authenticity, asset rights and no fake public figures/news graphics. DTCG semantic roles: \`background, subject, highlight, danger, positive, text, evidence\`; contrast and separation against light/dark YouTube UI. No assumption that red arrows, shock faces or neon outperform elegant editorial design.

Produce three **different concepts**, e.g. \`visible-cost-reversal\` (one price → total cost), \`mechanism-cutaway\` (inside a roof/HVAC), \`decision-comparison\` (two alternatives + break-even). Render 16:9 and previews at 160×90 and 320×180 (heuristics) with exact loaded fonts; validate occupied area, no accidental text clipping/overlap and salient subject. Human selects expressive quality; automatic geometry check cannot rank "most viral."

YouTube currently offers eligible channels up to **three title/thumbnail variants** in concurrent platform-native A/B tests, judged by **watch-time share**; not available for Shorts (see [official help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)). Never call a CTR proxy the official winner, and never infer testing outcome without the owner's actual Studio results.

## 4. Hook and narrative compiler

**Long-form:**
- **0–5 seconds**: show recognizable subject, vivid consequence, contradiction, test or surprising result. An intriguing first sentence is useful; a real visual example is stronger than ornamental kinetic text.
- **5–30 seconds**: define the central question, demonstrate the price of ignorance or payoff, show credible evidence/objects; substantively fulfill the thumbnail/title promise. Do not waste first 20 seconds on a branded bumper or generic channel introduction.
- **Next**: orient with necessary baseline; expose mechanism as causal transitions. A 12–25-minute explainer should earn its length through facts/tests rather than arbitrary scene churn.
- **End**: answer the question with conditions/caveats, actionable takeaway and optional next curiosity question. Do not replace conclusion with generic "subscribe."

These times are **creative starting assumptions**, not universal thresholds. YouTube explicitly measures retention after the **first 30 seconds** and reports dips/spikes in [Audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415).

**Short-form:** start with a comprehensible visible object/result in the first 0–2 seconds *when it helps*; give one concrete question and fast visual proof. Editorial heuristics for a 20–50s explainers, not mandatory cut counts:
- \`instant-result → rewind → cause → payoff\`
- \`common-belief → test → counterexample → qualified truth\`
- \`price-reveal → component-cost waterfall → total → condition\`
- \`A vs B → highlight threshold → surprising winner → caveat\`
- \`familiar-object → sectional cutaway → causal transfer → answer\`
- \`one mistake → practical consequence → fix → verification\`
- \`historical impossibility → failed attempt → breakthrough → modern impact\`

TikTok 2026's own [forecast](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/introducing-tiktok-next-2026-our-trend-forecast-for-marketers-for-the-year-ahead-ca?lang=en-CA) emphasizes authenticity, curiosity-driven discovery and useful context, **not** that all clips need flashing cuts. A polished technical channel can move more slowly while showing a powerful concrete change.

**Long-form story families:** \`mystery → competing explanations → experiment/evidence → resolution\`; \`quoted price → hidden line items → cash-flow simulation → fair comparison\`; \`failure → mechanism → competing engineering fixes → tradeoff\`; \`common belief → counterexample → investigate constraints → updated mental model\`. Each beat records \`setup, known facts, key object, transformation, changed state, narrationCue, evidenceRefs, unresolvedQuestion, expectedPayoff\`.

### Storytelling integrity guards

- Open loop is allowed only when later closed; each essential unresolved question must have a payoff ID and an ending.
- Each causal beat must declare **input → mechanism/transformation → visible result**. If the visuals would be decorative without text, revise the visual metaphor.
- Do not manufacture suspense with false danger or incorrect financial forecasts. Use uncertainty and ranges where supported.
- Visual continuity uses same semantic IDs across scenes; camera movement follows a named subject and must protect critical UI/text objects.
- Controlled audiovisual variety is intentional: a meaningful reveal, comparison, chart, silence, or hold; do not use cuts every N seconds as a "retention algorithm."
- Long and short films need genuinely adapted narrative and portrait composition. A short is not long-form sped up/cropped.

## 5. Output contracts and Kumiki mapping

ContentGen owns the creative contracts: \`OpportunityBrief\`, \`PackagingHypothesis\`, \`TitleVariant\`, \`ThumbnailVariant\`, \`HookContract\`, \`StoryPlan\`, \`FeedbackObservation\`. These are *content/intent*, never competing visual transform/keyframe/scene transport formats.

Existing Kumiki components supply realization:
- BrandKit/DTCG → \`--km-*\` semantic palette, pinned fonts, motion/sonic mood.
- Header/metric/diagram/compare/cost waterfall → \`registry/infographic\` and \`registry/domains\` assets; use active capabilities only.
- Object identity, changes, reveals and causality → \`registry/motion\` MG1, VT1, MotionDirector, progressive-reveal, ProcessJourney.
- Framing and no-clipping → CC1, \`registry/visual\` critical subject bounds/protected text and output-size capture guards.
- Thumbnail → editable Kumiki media stills, SVG export, tiny-size preview, rights and contrast checks.
- Audio/timing → ContentGen's provider-neutral TTS, alignment and mix; named cues map to Kumiki's existing absolute-frame clock.
- Video → implemented \`VideoExportStage\` / local Playwright+FFmpeg; source PNG-per-frame exporter is not yet scalable without benchmarking.

No invented Kumiki \`Composition\` functions: [14](14-kumiki-integration.md) distinguishes implemented APIs from proposed wire contracts.

## 6. Outcome learning — no fake viral score

**Before publish:** record 3 package hypotheses, audience promise, source support, brand, estimated production effort, title/thumbnail variant IDs, date, current format, and whether creator approved. **After publish:** optionally import authorized YouTube Studio impressions, CTR, AVD, 30s retention, total watch time, first 24h/7d/28d views, traffic source, Shorts viewed-vs-swiped/engaged viewing where available, returning viewers, RPM/qualified revenue and A/B winner/watch-time share. Store denominators, dates, definitions and missing fields; public competitor stats cannot reveal these private metrics.

Learn separately:
- \`impressions → clicks\`: packaging and subject affinity, qualified by audience/source
- \`clicks → 30s hold\`: hook-to-title promise match
- \`30s → narrative retention\`: visual evidence, pacing, information structure
- \`watch time / RPM / production hours\`: business sustainability
- \`short discovery → long-form returning audience\`: hypothesis to test, not guaranteed funnel.

Prefer platform-native simultaneous tests to sequential publishing changes. For Shorts, compare matched historical windows/cohorts and accept lower causal confidence; YouTube title/thumbnail A/B test tool does not support Shorts. A result with small samples or inconclusive native test remains inconclusive.

**Avoid proxy gaming:** multiple AI-written videos with near-identical wording/thumbnails are not "original variants." If an agent produces 15 generic copies, fail **distinctiveness** and human editorial review. See YouTube [monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) and Meta's [original creator guidance](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/).

## 7. Small vertical slice / acceptance fixtures

1. Research 3 similar channels in a defined 90-day/format window, identify their median, public outliers and underperformers; record exactly when metrics were observed.
2. Given a source-backed "real 30-year cost of a $500k house" brief, produce **three structurally distinct** packaging hypotheses and a real model of mortgage interest, insurance, taxes and maintenance under stated assumptions. No absolute lifetime number unless input evidence/assumptions justify it.
3. Compile a 25–40s portrait mechanism and a 5–10min landscape adaptation using Kumiki's existing diagram/infographic assets, with meaningful speech cues and calm/energetic SoundMood overrides that preserve BrandKit.
4. Fail seeded defects: title promises absent from content; no payoff; missing evidence for quantified thumbnail claim; duplicate variants; unlicensed illustration; first reveal occurs before narration or relevant visual; essential object cropped; long-video layout blindly cropped to portrait.
5. Simulate incoming analytics with unknown values, inconclusive YouTube A/B outcome and Shorts lacking native title/thumbnail test. No claimed virality from heuristic scores.
6. Report QA plus human preview points, measured runtime/disk/cost, known uncertainties and one revised creative hypothesis. No Codex/Work/paid services without explicit instruction.

**Agent instruction:** read the dated evidence snapshot separately; optimize for user comprehension and credible watch time, choose *fewer and better* cinematic devices, reuse Kumiki primitive contracts, and return edit-ready source + provenance rather than generic "viral" render effects.
